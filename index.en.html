device fingerprinting, explained

What your browser knows about you

No site needs to ask your name to recognize you next time. Your browser already tells it everything — no cookies, no login required. Here are seven things it usually reveals, what you can do about each, and at the end you'll see exactly what yours exposes.

01

Public IP address

Every server you connect to can see the address you're coming from. From it, your approximate location (usually city or region) and internet provider can be inferred. It's a necessary part of how the internet works — without it, the response wouldn't know where to go.

How to limit it

A VPN or Tor replaces your IP with the server you're connecting through. Keep in mind this only hides this one item — the rest of the signals below work entirely independently of your IP.

02

Local IP via WebRTC

WebRTC (the technology for in-browser video calls) sometimes exchanges local network addresses between peers when establishing a connection. In practice, this means a site can discover your local IP (e.g. 192.168.x.x), and in some cases even your real public IP even when using a VPN — because WebRTC runs outside the VPN tunnel.

How to limit it

Firefox: about:config → media.peerconnection.enabled set to false (disables WebRTC entirely, may break video calls). Chrome/Brave: extensions like "WebRTC Leak Prevent", or in Brave enable stricter fingerprinting protection in shield settings.

03

Canvas fingerprint

A site makes your browser render an invisible image (text, shapes) onto an HTML5 canvas and reads the resulting data pixel by pixel. The combination of GPU, drivers, and operating system renders this image slightly differently each time — and that tiny difference works as a fingerprint that doesn't need cookies.

How to limit it

Firefox has built-in privacy.resistFingerprinting (in about:config), Tor Browser has it enabled by default and additionally randomizes canvas output. Brave does the same automatically. Extensions like CanvasBlocker also exist.

04

Audio fingerprint

Same principle as canvas, but with sound: your browser generates and processes a short audio waveform via the Web Audio API, and tiny deviations in the output (caused by hardware and software) form another stable fingerprint — you hear nothing, the whole thing happens silently in the background.

How to limit it

The same measures as for canvas (resistFingerprinting, Tor Browser, Brave) typically also cover the audio fingerprint — both techniques attack a similar layer of the browser.

05

Installed fonts

A site renders text in dozens of different fonts and measures their width. If a font is missing, the browser falls back to a substitute and the width difference is noticeable. The set of installed fonts tends to be quite specific to a particular computer — especially for professions that install lots of fonts (graphic designers, typesetters).

How to limit it

Tor Browser normalizes the font set across all users, so this signal stops being useful. Outside Tor there's no simple defense — it's just a matter of not installing more fonts than you need.

06

GPU via WebGL/WebGPU

Your browser can reveal the make and model of your graphics card (e.g. "NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3060") on request — usually for game and video optimization, but it's also another nearly unique piece of data about your device.

How to limit it

Firefox: webgl.disabled in about:config (breaks sites that need WebGL — games, maps, 3D visualizations). Tor Browser blocks WebGL by default.

07

Combined "device ID"

None of the above signals may be unique on its own — lots of people have the same graphics card. But combine GPU + canvas hash + audio hash + fonts + timezone + resolution together, and suddenly you have a fingerprint shared by almost nobody else. That's exactly what the "device ID" below does.

How to limit it

No single measure disables the whole thing — the strength is in the combination. The most effective defense is a browser that standardizes as many signals as possible equally for all its users (Tor Browser is furthest along here), so you stop standing out from everyone else.
